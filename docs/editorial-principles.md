# HETRICH public copy

Owner direction, 2026-10-23: let visitors discover the value of experience and judgment. Internal project status should not dominate the public story.

## Lead with evidence and usefulness

- Explain the situation, the decision, the implementation, and what was learned. Keep concrete examples, measured conditions, and actual collaborator roles.
- Present development notes as useful experience. A future publication can be announced when there is a publication to announce; a pause, provisional title, or missing release date does not need a public status card.
- Describe trial and error through the judgment it produced. Do not turn it into a confession, erase the learning process, or imply success and authorship beyond the evidence.
- State previous organizational experience positively and explicitly. The developer's contribution and the collaborators' responsibilities establish scope without repeated disclaimers about studio commissions.

## Separate review notes from visitor information

- Keep source audits, unverified rollout questions, and unimplemented proposals in evidence documents. Avoid automatically copying them into product descriptions.
- Describe verified capabilities. Do not imply an unverified capability merely by deleting its caveat. HIS remains a development version, with login requirements and device-notification verification status stated briefly.
- Preserve information that affects use: estimated nutrition values, historical game data, unofficial/noncommercial fan-work status, actual chapter unlock rules, and actionable loading or form errors.
- Screenshots and feedback summaries retain their provenance. Historical performance numbers retain measurement conditions.
- Do not publish unreleased concepts or their mechanics without an explicit request to make them public.

## Style

Use concrete verbs and concise explanations. Avoid repeated apologies, internal approval terminology, defensive qualifications, unsupported superlatives, and treating aspirations as delivered features. Keep the founder's curiosity and willingness to experiment visible.
